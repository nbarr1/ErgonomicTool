## What the method assesses

The Shoulder Tool estimates a daily dose of cumulative damage (CD) to a shoulder from repeated loading, and the probability of a shoulder outcome from that dose. The sources describe it in these terms:

- **Purpose:** "The tool estimates cumulative damage (CD) based on shoulder moments and loading cycles using an S–N curve derived from in vitro tendon fatigue failure tests. If multiple shoulder tasks are performed, the CD for each is summed." (abstract, PDF p. 2 (journal p. 39)). Section 4 describes a tool that "provides a CD measure for the shoulder representing cumulative loading on the shoulders during a workday" (PDF p. 9 (journal p. 46)).
- **Tissue basis:** "Tendons of the shoulder are musculoskeletal tissues of particular concern" (PDF p. 4 (journal p. 41)). The damage model uses an S–N curve from tests of "ninety specimens of the human extensor digitorum longus (EDL) tendon" (PDF p. 4 (journal p. 41)).
- **Outcome:** "The Shoulder Tool provides the probability of a clinic visit for a shoulder MSD associated with a particular job" (PDF p. 9 (journal p. 46)). The workbook labels its result "Probability of Shoulder Outcome:" (TST_v1.0.0!D35, line 18), and its coefficients are those of the dissertation's first time office visit (FTOV) model. Which outcome the result names is OQ-201.
- **Body region:** Upper limb. The source's term is "shoulder." The controlled vocabulary maps it to the region "upper limb."
- **Body side:** Right or left. The paper says "The tool provides analysis for each shoulder separately, thus the greatest lever arm for one shoulder does not need to happen at the same time as the other one." (PDF p. 9 (journal p. 46)). The workbook computes one shoulder at a time: its calculator sheet has no shoulder input, and its saved analyses record "Left" or "Right" in a "Shoulder" column (TST_v1.0.0_database, column G, read once). How the application handles the two shoulders is OQ-202.
- **Job types:** Monotask jobs (section 4.1), "jobs with multiple shoulder tasks" (section 4.2), and "highly variable shoulder jobs" through a binning procedure (section 4.3) (PDF pp. 9-13 (journal pp. 46-50)).
- **Intended use:** "The Shoulder Tool can be used for both comparative redesign purposes and as a screening tool." (PDF p. 14 (journal p. 51)).

### Development and validation basis

The paper develops and validates the tool on one epidemiology database. These facts bound how a result can be read:

- **Database:** "The epidemiology study was executed in six different plant sites of a large US automotive manufacturer over the period of February 1998 to September 1999 (Sesek 1999). A total of 1022 subjects participated with male subjects representing 72.7% of the workforce." Ages were "20–70 years (41.0 ± 10.9 years SD)," heights ranged "from 147 to 203 cm (174.8 ± 9.4 cm SD)," and weights were "between 45 and 159 kg (84.8 ± 17.7 kg SD)" (PDF p. 4 (journal p. 41)). Workers were included if they "had been on a cyclic job for more than 30 days and agreed to be filmed" (PDF p. 4 (journal p. 41)).
- **Jobs and tasks:** "Two hundred sixteen randomly selected jobs were included in the analysis, which consisted of a total of 446 tasks. Jobs analysed were generally multitask in nature, with up to six tasks per job." (PDF p. 4 (journal p. 41)).
- **Exposure assessment:** "Video analysis was used to obtain postural estimates and the number of repetitions by measuring the duration between the start of a cycle of a job to the start of the subsequent cycle." "The most awkward shoulder posture for a task was used for postural analysis where the neutral posture was defined as arms hanging straight down by the side of the torso." (PDF p. 4 (journal p. 41)). Shoulder moments came from simulations in "3DSSPP version 6.0.6": "Both the postural estimates and the loads carried were used for moment calculations." (PDF p. 5 (journal p. 42)). The development moments are 3DSSPP estimates, not the workbook's load × lever arm plus arm moment, and no source compares the two.
- **Validation sample:** "In the validation, 293 workers were evaluated for five separate shoulder outcomes." (PDF p. 2 (journal p. 39)). The outcomes are pain today, pain last year, the job-level first time office visit (FTOV) record, and two contrasts of job attribution, "1,2 vs. 4,5" and "1 vs. 5" (PDF p. 7 (journal p. 44); Table 3, PDF p. 9 (journal p. 46)). "First Time Office Visits (FTOVs) for shoulder/neck symptoms were also reported." (PDF p. 4 (journal p. 41)). "To be considered a case, the VAS score had to exceed 15 mm" (PDF p. 7 (journal p. 44)).
- **Covariates:** "Sex, age, plant site and body mass index (BMI) were included as covariates for the adjusted binary logistic regression model." For FTOV, "only plant site was included in the analysis as a covariate." (PDF p. 7 (journal p. 44)).
- **Exposure and outcome on different workers:** "It is important to note that when multiple workers completed the same job, the subject reporting the injury was not necessarily the same subject observed during exposure assessment." (PDF p. 4 (journal p. 41)).

Two printed inconsistencies in the paper's validation results don't affect the calculation. They're recorded for the reviewer:

- The abstract says the odds ratios "ranged from 2.12 to 5.20" (PDF p. 2 (journal p. 39)), and the conclusion says "ranged from 2.12 to 5.19" (PDF p. 15 (journal p. 52)). Table 3 prints 5.20 (PDF p. 9 (journal p. 46)).
- Table 2 gives 47 cases for pain today (PDF p. 8 (journal p. 45)), and Table 3 gives 46 (PDF p. 9 (journal p. 46)).

## Applicability and scope checks

The paper names three loading conditions that the tool doesn't account for. Part 2 of the build prompt says "When a task falls outside a method's stated scope, say so and don't calculate," and it uses an arm held over the shoulder in the Shoulder Tool as its example. This specification therefore proposes that the assessor answers each condition yes or no for every task, and that the engine doesn't calculate a task with a yes answer:

| Condition recorded by the assessor                                                                            | Source wording (PDF p. 15 (journal p. 52))                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The arm is held straight in line with the shoulder, so that the force passes through it, as in a forward push | "it does not account for all loading conditions such as when an arm is held straight in line with the shoulder such as a push forward action when stress in the shoulder will be generated even though no moment is present"             |
| The arm is held straight up over the shoulder                                                                 | "Another example is when the arm is held straight above the shoulder. Although a low moment is created, intensive muscle contraction is taking place and this loading condition can contribute to physiological (and material) fatigue." |
| The task involves continuous overhead work                                                                    | "Also, the tool does not account for the additional load associated with continuous overhead work."                                                                                                                                      |

These points support the checks:

- Eq 2 defines the total as "the daily dose for the CD for all tasks" (PDF p. 4 (journal p. 41)). A total that leaves out an out-of-scope task would understate that dose, so the proposal also withholds the total CD and the probability for that shoulder, and names the task that stopped the calculation. The reviewer confirms or changes this proposal under OQ-203.
- "To estimate the CD, data collection is needed to obtain a complete job description" (PDF p. 15 (journal p. 52)). The total covers only the tasks the assessor enters.
- The workbook's instructions describe the first two conditions in substance and stop short of the third (Instructions!B158, read once). The paper's wording governs.

### Arm moment

The workbook's arm moment is this formula, under the heading "Arm Moment (ft. lbs)" (TST_v1.0.0!R3, line 10):

```text
IF(OR(D5="Please select...", F5="", H5="", L5=""), "", IF(F5<1.715, 0, IF(F5>=27, 121.9/12, (-8.27+4.8223*F5)/12)))        (TST_v1.0.0!R5, line 11)
```

Written out, with F the lever arm in inches and the arm moment in foot-pounds:

```text
ArmMoment = 0                          when F < 1.715
ArmMoment = (−8.27 + 4.8223 × F) / 12  when 1.715 ≤ F < 27
ArmMoment = 121.9 / 12                 when F ≥ 27
```

The hidden sheet push_pull states the same rule in words: `If lever arm<1.715` with `0.0`, `If lever arm>=1.715 and <27` with `(-8.27 + 4.8223 * lever_arm)/12.0` followed by a non-breaking space, and `If lever arm>=27` with `121.9 / 12.0` (push_pull!B10:C12, lines 30 to 35). These points apply to the arm moment:

- **What it represents:** The paper's Table 4 note says "Moment includes a moment due to the weight of the forearm and hand based on lever arm." (PDF p. 13 (journal p. 50)), and the Figure 11 caption says "Note that The Shoulder Tool adds a moment due to the forearm and hand weight based on lever arm." (PDF p. 14 (journal p. 51)). No source states the segment weights or the origin of 1.715, 27, −8.27, 4.8223, or 121.9. Both paper passes recorded "not printed" for a formula (key `forearm-moment:formula`), and the dissertation prints none.
- **Continuity:** Recomputed by the build, the linear branch gives 0.0002445/12 ft·lb at 1.715 in and 121.9321/12 ft·lb at 27 in. The rule is continuous at 1.715 in to within 0.00003 ft·lb and drops 0.0027 ft·lb at 27 in.
- **Metric size:** Recomputed by the build, the arm moment is 5.50 N·m at 30 cm, 5.72 at 31 cm, 7.22 at 38 cm, 7.86 at 41 cm, 8.93 at 46 cm, and 10.01 at 51 cm. These agree within 0.06 N·m with the excess of the paper's printed moments over load × lever arm that draft 1 of this specification recorded (5.50 to 10.03 N·m for Table 4).
- **Blank test:** R5 tests "Please select..." without the space that the list entry and the other formulas use (finding F2). It doesn't change any result.

### Moment

The workbook's moment is this formula, under the heading "Moment \n(ft.lbs.)" (TST_v1.0.0!J3, line 6):

```text
IF(OR(D5="Please select ...",F5="",H5="", L5=""),"", IF(VLOOKUP(D5,push_pull!$B$2:$B$5,1,FALSE)="Horizontal Push/Pull", F5/12*H5,IF(VLOOKUP(D5,push_pull!$B$2:$B$5,1,FALSE)="Handling Load",F5/12*H5+(R5),F5/12*H5-(R5))))        (TST_v1.0.0!J5, line 12)
```

Written out, with H the load in pounds and M the moment in foot-pounds:

```text
M = F / 12 × H + ArmMoment   for "Handling Load"
M = F / 12 × H               for "Horizontal Push/Pull"
M = F / 12 × H − ArmMoment   for any other listed type, which is "Push/Pull Downward"
```

These points apply to the moment:

- **Blank rule:** The moment is blank while the type reads "Please select ..." or the lever arm, the load, or the repetitions are blank.
- **Negative moments:** Recomputed by the build, "Push/Pull Downward" gives a negative moment whenever the arm moment exceeds load × lever arm. For example, 12 in and 1 lb give 1 − 4.1331 = −3.1331 ft·lb. The workbook doesn't stop it (OQ-203).
- **Paper:** The paper's one "Horizontal Push or Pull" row (Figure 10, task 3, PDF p. 13 (journal p. 50)) has a moment equal to load × lever arm, and every "Handling Loads" row in Figures 8 to 11 has a larger one (recomputed by the build), as this rule gives. Recomputed by the build, the rule reproduces the paper's Table 4 and Figures 8 to 11 to within 0.06 N·m. See "Worked examples for the published-example tests."
- **Screenshots:** Recomputed by the build, the rule reproduces every moment in the dissertation's Figures A.5 to A.8 and the workbook's instruction screenshots to the printed 0.1 ft·lb.

### Damage for each task

The workbook's damage is this formula, under the heading "Damage\n(cum.)" (TST_v1.0.0!N3, line 8). The task-10 formula in N32 is the same with row 32 references (line 15):

```text
IF(OR(F5="", H5="", L5="", D5="Please select ..."),"", IF(H5=0,0,1/(10^((101.25-100*(J5*12/681))/14.83))*L5))        (TST_v1.0.0!N5, line 13)
```

Written out, with S the stress as a percentage of the ultimate strength, N the cycles to failure, and L the repetitions per workday:

```text
S = 100 × (M × 12 / 681)
N = 10^((101.25 − S) / 14.83)
Damage per cycle = 1 / N
```
