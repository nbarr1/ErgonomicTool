## What the method assesses

DUET estimates a daily dose of cumulative damage (CD) to the distal upper extremity from repeated exertions, and the probability of one distal upper extremity outcome from that dose. The source describes the method in these terms:

- **Purpose:** "DUET is designed to provide a measure of daily exposure to DUE risk factors and can evaluate mono-task jobs or exposures associated with multiple tasks. The tool requires only two inputs per task: a force exertion assessment and the number of repetitions performed" (PDF p. 2). The abstract adds: "DUET requires an estimate of force exertion level and the number of repetitions performed to derive estimates of damage and probabilities of experiencing a distal upper extremity outcome. Damage accrued over multiple tasks may be summed to estimate the cumulative damage (CD) accrued over a workday" (PDF p. 1).
- **Tissue basis:** "In DUE MSDs, tendons are a tissue of primary concern." The damage weighting uses data from "a study of fatigue failure of the human extensor digitorum longus at different levels of ultimate tensile strength (Schechtman & Bader, 1997)" (PDF p. 2).
- **Body region:** Upper limb. The source's term is "distal upper extremity (DUE)" (PDF p. 1). The source doesn't define the term by anatomical structure. The controlled vocabulary maps it to the region "upper limb."
- **Body side:** Not stated by the source.
- **Outputs:** Each task's CD and its percentage of the total, the job's total CD, and a probability. See "Outputs."

The source prints risk-model coefficients for one outcome only. The abstract says: "A model relating the continuous DUET log CD score to the probability of the DUE outcome Injury + Pain Last Year was developed, which demonstrated a significant dose-response relationship" (PDF p. 1). The outcome is defined from the validation study, in which "Injury was defined as a job where a first-time office visit (FTOV) for DUE symptoms occurred during the past year" and, for the combined outcomes, "participants on jobs having a recorded FTOV during the past year plus participant reported pain (>15 mm visual analog scale [VAS] current or past year) were considered cases, and individuals on jobs without an FTOV plus no current pain or pain last year (<15 mm VAS) were controls" (PDF p. 4).

The paper words the same result in other ways: "an estimated probability of 26.5% of experiencing a DUE disorder" (PDF p. 11), "the DUE risk" (PDF p. 12), and, in the calculator screenshots, "Probability of Distal Upper Extremity Outcome (%)" (Figures 2 and 3, PDF p. 13). The workbook uses the figures' label (DUET_v1.3.0!B35, single reading). Which label the application shows is open (OQ-190).

- "To validate the DUET CD metric, we used data from an epidemiological study involving a large automotive manufacturer (Sesek, 1999)." ### Repetitions

The validation study counted hand activities: "All jobs were videotaped, and the analysis team reviewed hand activities (grips and deviations) by randomly sampling videotaped jobs. Teams of two to three trained analysts agreed on a consensus count of task motions" (PDF p. 6). "For this analysis, three definitions of repetition were analyzed, including deviations alone, grips alone, and deviations+grips" (PDF p. 7). The risk model of Eq 6 comes from the analysis "Using Total Grips and Deviations" (Table 5 title, PDF p. 10). The paper also says the results "may provide analysts or researchers flexibility in terms of how repetitions are defined" (PDF pp. 12-13). The source doesn't say whether the two hands are counted separately. The workbook asks for "the number of repetitions of the task during the workday" without defining a repetition (Instructions!B13, single reading), and its input check accepts whole numbers of at least 0 in each repetitions cell (DUET_v1.3.0!F5 and the other task rows, single reading). What counts as a repetition, and which values are allowed, is open (OQ-193).

### Deriving repetitions from a rate

The mono-task example derives the daily count from a rate: "The estimated CD can be calculated by multiplying the number of efforts/minute (10) by 60 (minutes/hour) and then multiplying the resulting product by the total hours worked (9) to arrive at the total daily repetitions (5,400)" (PDF pp. 10-11).

```text
n = efforts/minute × 60 × total hours worked        (PDF pp. 10-11)
```

Proposed: the form offers this route as an alternative to entering n. The application stores the rate and the hours with the provenance "derived," and computes n first and the task CD second, in the order the mono-task example uses. Table 3 applies the same rate route through damage per hour (PDF p. 7), and its rates give counts that aren't whole numbers. Recomputed by the build: 5.4545 efforts per minute over 10 hours is 3,272.7 repetitions. The workbook has no rate route, and its input check rejects a count that isn't a whole number (single reading). Whether such counts are accepted is part of OQ-193.

## Equations

The calculation has four steps, each described in its own subsection:

1. Find the damage per cycle (DPC) for each task's rating.
2. Multiply it by the task's repetitions to get the task CD.
3. Sum the task CDs to get the total CD, and compute each task's percentage of the total.
4. Compute the probability of the outcome from the total CD.

The workbook computes the same steps. This table maps each quantity to its source in the paper and its workbook cell; the subsections give each workbook formula as stored, with its transcription line:

### Damage per cycle

The source derives the DPC weights from a tendon fatigue relationship: "relationship of the percentage of ultimate stress level versus median fatigue life was expressed by the following equation" (PDF p. 3):

```text
S = 101.25 − 14.83 × log(N)        (PDF p. 3, Eq 1)
```

"where S represents the percentage of tensile stress (relative to ultimate tensile stress), and N represents cycles to failure" (PDF p. 3). The source scales the OMNI-RES rating to stress with this rule: "Thus, we scaled cycles to failure such that the one cycle failure value (i.e., ultimate stress) was 1.4 times the maximum exertion force on the OMNI-RES scale (Table 1)" (PDF p. 3). It defines the DPC in these words: "Based on this relationship, the DPC for various levels of stress was determined. DPC represents the proportion of damage occurring in one cycle at a given stress level. These data were related to force levels of the OMNI-RES scale to provide a weighting for the effect of repetition at various levels of force (see Table 1)" (PDF p. 3).

The source prints no formula that gives S from a rating, no formula that gives the DPC from N, and no base for the logarithm. The build checked Table 1 against Eq 1, with these results, all recomputed by the build:

- With a base-10 logarithm, every printed cycles-to-failure value equals 10^((101.25 − S)/14.83) rounded to an integer, using the printed one-decimal S. A natural logarithm doesn't reproduce the table: at S = 71.4 it gives N of about 7.5, where Table 1 prints 103.
- Every printed DPC equals 1/N rounded to eight decimal places, using the printed N.
- For ratings 1 to 10, the printed S equals the rating × 100/14 rounded to one decimal place. Row 0 (3.6) isn't explained.
- Computing N from the unrounded S doesn't reproduce the table. For rating 1, S = 100/14 gives N = 2,216,806, where Table 1 prints 2,231,607.

## Limitations to show on every result
